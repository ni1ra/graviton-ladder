# The ladder theorem: final statement, proof, completeness argument and referee attack

Lane w69-final (Fable), Tuesday 10 November 2026, Oslo. Claude only. Nothing is priced, booked or committed here.

**Result in four lines.**
- For free linearised gravity on the flat unit ball and a stationary magnetic multipole K of degree l ≥ 2, the relative entropy of the coherent state to the vacuum is exactly **S = ((l+1)/(l+2))·A/8G**, with A = ∫_B N|K|², N = (1 − r²)/2.

- On every vacuum solution of Einstein's equations with that first-order part, the extremal value of (Area − 2·Volume)/4G drops by exactly **A/8G** at second order.
- So the second-order first law of the ball misses by **A/((l+2)·8G)**, for every l ≥ 2. The two integers are the rates of two flows: the modular flow moves the electric curvature by (l+1)K, the conformal Killing flow of the metric by (l+2)K.

## 1. Setting

**Units and conventions.** ħ = c = 1; G is kept. Space indices i, j; r = |x|; n = x/r; B the open unit ball in the slice t = 0; S = ∂B; O = D(B) its causal diamond; O′ the causal complement. (curl T)_ij = ε_ikl ∂_k T_lj; (x × T)_ij = ε_ikl x_k T_lj; sym is the symmetric part.

**One-particle space.** ℋ is the space of pairs u = (h, p) of transverse traceless (TT) symmetric tensor fields on ℝ³, h the metric perturbation and p = ½∂_t h, completed in ‖h‖²_{Ḣ^{1/2}} + ‖2p‖²_{Ḣ^{−1/2}} (times 1/64πG). Time evolution is ∂_t h = 2p, ∂_t p = ½Δh. Its imaginary part is half the canonical symplectic form of the Einstein–Hilbert action:

  Im⟨u, u′⟩ = (1/32πG) ∫ (h·p′ − h′·p).

The complex structure is the positive-energy one (wave70 §1: multiplication by i on the wave function ω^{1/2}ĥ + 2iω^{−1/2}p̂). The inner product is antilinear in the first slot. Up to the constant 1/64πG and the factor 2 in p, this is the space of wave69 and wave70; duality and the modular group do not see either constant.

**Curvature.** E[u] = −½Δh, the linearised Ricci tensor of the slice (for any symmetric h with ∂_i∂_j h_ij − Δh_kk = 0, Ric₃(h) = E of its TT part). B[u] = −curl p. Both are TT, ∂_t E = −curl B, ∂_t B = curl E. This is Paper A's B; wave69 and wave70 use −B.

**Net.** V(u) are the Weyl unitaries on the Bose Fock space 𝔉 over ℋ, in the convention of Ciolli–Longo–Ruzzi: V(u)V(u′) = e^{−i Im⟨u,u′⟩}V(u + u′), ⟨Ω, V(u)Ω⟩ = e^{−‖u‖²/2}, V(u)Ω = e^{−‖u‖²/2}Exp(u). For a real test field F on spacetime with values in pairs of symmetric traceless tensors, e_F ∈ ℋ is the vector with Im⟨u, e_F⟩ ∝ ∫F·(E, B)[u_t]: the curvature of u smeared with F. For an open region U, H(U) is the closed real span of {e_F : supp F ⊂ U}, R(H) = {V(u) : u ∈ H}″ and 𝒜(U) = R(H(U)). 𝒜(O′) is generated by the 𝒜(O₁), O₁ a double cone spacelike to O. ω_u = ⟨V(u)Ω, · V(u)Ω⟩ is the coherent state of u; ω₀ the vacuum.

**Flow.** ζ = ½(1 − t² − r²)∂_t − t x·∂ is the conformal Killing vector of O; ζ = N∂_t on the slice, N = (1 − r²)/2. On curvature at t = 0:

  δ_ζE = N∂_tE + 2 sym(x × B),  δ_ζB = N∂_tB − 2 sym(x × E).  (1)

**The scope family.** Let φ be a harmonic polynomial, homogeneous of degree l ≥ 2. Put v = x × ∇φ and

  K = sym ∇v.

The first-order metric perturbation is h⁽¹⁾ = 2tK: Cauchy data (h, p) = (0, K) near B̄. Its curvature is (E, B) = (0, −curl K), independent of t. Put

  A = ∫_B N |K|².

**Facts about K, for every l** (each line is homogeneity plus x·v = 0, div v = 0, Δv = 0; checked exactly at l = 2..5 on four harmonics each, `s1` M1–M4):
- (K1) K is transverse, traceless and harmonic; curl K is symmetric.
- (K2) K·x = ((l−1)/2)·v, so x·K·x = 0: K_nn vanishes on every sphere.
- (K3) 2 sym(x × B) = (l+1)K. Proof: (x × curl K)_ij = x_k(∂_iK_kj − ∂_kK_ij) = ∂_i(K·x)_j − K_ij − (l−1)K_ij; symmetrise with (K2).
- (K4) Ric₃(2NK) = (l+2)K. Proof: Ric₃(h) = ½(−Δh + ∂_i(div h)_j + ∂_j(div h)_i − ∂_i∂_j tr h); Δ((1−r²)K) = −(4l+2)K; div((1−r²)K) = −2K·x = −(l−1)v.
- (K5) A = ∮_S|K|² / ((2l+1)(2l+3)).
- (K6) With wave69's Cotton–York operator C, C(r²K) = −2(l+2) curl K (`s1` M8).

## 3. The theorem

**Theorem (the ladder).** Let l ≥ 2 and K be as in Section 1.

**(M) Modular side.** There are vectors k ∈ ℋ whose curvature on O is that of h⁽¹⁾ = 2tK; the restriction of ω_k to 𝒜(O) is the same for all of them; and

  S_{𝒜(O)}(ω_k‖ω₀) = ((l+1)/(l+2)) · A/8G.

This is exact: S is a quadratic form in K, with no higher order.

**(C) Every admissible algebra.** If (M, Φ, ι) obeys H1–H3, then S_M(ω_k∘Φ‖ω₀∘Φ) = S_{𝒜(O)}(ω_k‖ω₀), for every k ∈ ℋ.

**(B) Area side.** Let g_ε obey H5 with h⁽¹⁾ = 2tK + £_ξη, ξ any smooth vector field. Then the coordinate ball, moved by the flow of −εξ, is stationary for F[g_ε; ·] to first order in ε, and at every configuration within O(ε) of the coordinate ball that is stationary to first order,

  F = F₀ − (ε²/2)·A + o(ε²),  F₀ = 4π/3.

The value does not depend on h⁽²⁾, on ξ, or on which such configuration is taken. So under H4 the area side is ε²A/8G.

**(L) The ladder.** Put the amplitude ε into K on the modular side. Under H0–H5,

  area side − modular side = A/((l+2)·8G) at O(ε²),  modular side / area side = (l+1)/(l+2).

**(W) Why these integers.** Write a = (0, K) and Ω(a, b) = ∫(p_a·h_b − p_b·h_a). The conformal Killing flow moves the metric by δ_geo a = (2NK, 0), and A = ½Ω(a, δ_geo a). The modular flow moves the curvature by (1), which on a is (δE, δB) = ((l+1)K, 0); the metric perturbation in the ball algebra with that curvature is δ_mod a = ((l+1)/(l+2))·(2NK, 0), by (K4), and S = (1/8G)·½Ω(a, δ_mod a). Equivalently, with ψ = x·B·x and E_w[ψ] = ½∫_B N|∇ψ|² + (w/2)∫_B ψ², and c_l = 4(l−2)!/(l+2)!:

  A = c_l E₂[ψ],  8G·S = c_l E₁[ψ],  A − 8G·S = (c_l/2)∫_B ψ² = A/(l+2).

No other pair of weights fits, because E_w − E_{w′} = ((w − w′)/2)∫ψ² and ∫ψ² > 0.

**Scope.** d = 4; flat unit ball (any radius by dilation); l ≥ 2; stationary magnetic data, any mixture of m at fixed l. Not claimed: anything about the G → 0 limit; an electric ladder; d ≠ 4 or spin ≠ 2 (Paper B); that the physical ball algebra obeys H1–H3 or that the physical area side obeys H4.

**Extension E (from the record, not re-proved here).** For every smooth odd-parity datum of degree l, not only the stationary one, A_ext = c_l E₂[ψ] and 8G·S = c_l E₁[ψ] (companion A8 Theorem 2 and Proposition 4 with Paper A r18 Theorem 1). On such data only the transfer route gives S.

## 4. Second quantisation, proved

The print read found no proof on file of the facts below. Here is one. It rests on T6 and T7 only.

**Proposition SQ.** Let H ⊂ ℋ be a standard subspace (closed, real, H ∩ iH = {0}, H + iH dense), with S_H(x + iy) = x − iy = J_HΔ_H^{1/2}. Then:
- (a) H′ = J_H H, and H′ is standard.
- (b) Ω is cyclic and separating for R(H).
- (c) Δ_{R(H)}^{it} = Γ(Δ_H^{it}).
- (d) J_{R(H)} = Γ(−J_H), and R(H)′ = R(H′).
- (e) For closed real subspaces H_α, R(closed span of the H_α) = ∨R(H_α).

*Proof.*
(a) For h, k ∈ H: ⟨J_Hh, k⟩ = ⟨J_Hh, J_HΔ^{1/2}k⟩ = ⟨Δ^{1/2}k, h⟩ = ⟨k, Δ^{1/2}h⟩ = ⟨k, J_Hh⟩, so ⟨J_Hh, k⟩ is real and J_HH ⊂ H′. Conversely let h′ ∈ H′. For x = h₁ + ih₂: ⟨S_Hx, h′⟩ = ⟨h₁, h′⟩ + i⟨h₂, h′⟩ = ⟨h′, x⟩, since ⟨h_j, h′⟩ is real. So h′ ∈ Dom(S_H*) and S_H*h′ = h′. S_H* = J_HΔ_H^{−1/2}, and J_HΔ^{−1/2}y = y is the same as J_HΔ^{1/2}(J_Hy) = J_Hy, that is J_Hy ∈ H. So H′ ⊂ J_HH. A complex vector orthogonal to H′ + iH′ lies in H″ ∩ iH″ = H ∩ iH = {0}, and H′ ∩ iH′ is orthogonal to H + iH; so H′ is standard.
(b) Let 𝔐 be the closed complex span of {Exp(h) : h ∈ H}. For h₁, h₂ ∈ H the map z ↦ Exp(h₂ + zh₁) is entire into 𝔉 and lies in 𝔐 for real z, hence for all z (pair with 𝔐^⊥ and use the identity theorem). Repeating, Exp(g) ∈ 𝔐 for g in the complex span of H, which is dense; Exp is continuous and exponential vectors are total. So Ω is cyclic for R(H), and for R(H′) by (a). The Weyl relation gives V(h′)V(h) = V(h)V(h′) when Im⟨h, h′⟩ = 0, so R(H′) ⊂ R(H)′ and Ω is separating for R(H).
(c) U(t) = Γ(Δ_H^{it}) fixes Ω and U(t)V(h)U(t)* = V(Δ_H^{it}h), so σ_t = Ad U(t) maps R(H) onto itself (Δ_H^{it}H = H). The span 𝔄 of {V(h) : h ∈ H} is a σ-invariant *-algebra, σ-weakly dense in R(H). For h, k ∈ H put f(t) = ⟨h, Δ_H^{it}k⟩. Since h, k ∈ Dom(Δ_H^{1/2}), f(t + is) = ⟨Δ^{−s/2}h, Δ^{it}Δ^{−s/2}k⟩ is bounded and continuous on −1 ≤ s ≤ 0, analytic inside, and

  f(t − i) = ⟨Δ^{1/2}h, Δ^{it}Δ^{1/2}k⟩ = ⟨J_Hh, J_HΔ^{it}k⟩ = ⟨Δ^{it}k, h⟩.

The Weyl relation and the vacuum functional give ω₀(V(h)σ_t(V(k))) = c·e^{−f(t)} and ω₀(σ_t(V(k))V(h)) = c·e^{−⟨Δ^{it}k, h⟩}, c = e^{−(‖h‖²+‖k‖²)/2}. So F(t) = ω₀(Aσ_t(B)) continues to the strip with F(t − i) = ω₀(σ_t(B)A), first for Weyl operators and by linearity on 𝔄. That is the KMS condition at β = −1. By T7, σ is the modular group of (R(H), Ω); both unitary groups fix Ω, which is cyclic, so they are equal.
(d) By (c) and the functional calculus Δ_{R(H)}^{1/2} = Γ(Δ_H^{1/2}), and Exp(h) ∈ Dom Γ(Δ_H^{1/2}) with image Exp(Δ_H^{1/2}h) for h ∈ H. The Tomita operator sends V(h)Ω to V(−h)Ω, that is Exp(h) to Exp(−h) = Exp(−J_HΔ_H^{1/2}h) = Γ(−J_H)Γ(Δ_H^{1/2})Exp(h). So J_{R(H)} and Γ(−J_H) agree on Δ_{R(H)}^{1/2}𝔄Ω = J_{R(H)}𝔄Ω, which is dense; both are bounded. Γ(−J_H)V(h)Γ(−J_H)* = V(−J_Hh), so by T6 and (a), R(H)′ = J R(H) J = R(J_HH) = R(H′).
(e) ∨R(H_α) ⊂ R(span) is immediate. V(h₁ + h₂) is a phase times V(h₁)V(h₂), and u ↦ V(u) is strongly continuous, so V(u) ∈ ∨R(H_α) for u in the closed span. ∎

## 5. Proof of (M), direct route

Two record theorems are used.

**Theorem D (wave69/duality/REPORT.md §2).** H(O) = N(B̄), the vectors whose E and B vanish outside the closed ball; H(O)′ = N(ℝ³∖B), the vectors whose E and B vanish on the open ball; H(O′) = H(O)′. With Proposition SQ (d), (e): 𝒜(O)′ = 𝒜(O′).

**Theorem F (wave70/modflow/REPORT.md §2).** H(O) is standard, and Δ_{H(O)}^{it} e_F = e_{Λ_{−2πt*}F} for every real F ∈ C_c^∞(O), where Λ_s is the flow of ζ and Λ_{s*} the push-forward of test fields with conformal weight 3.

Both proofs were read here in full. Theorem F's Lemmas 4 and 5, flagged unchecked by their author, are re-derived in Section 10.

**M-a. The state.** Fix χ ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ³), radial, χ = 1 on B_{1+δ}. Put f = (2/(l+2))·χ r²K, a smooth compactly supported symmetric traceless field, and T = ¼Cf. T is smooth, TT and compactly supported, and by (K6) T = −curl K on B_{1+δ}. Let k ∈ ℋ be the vector with (E, B)[k] = (0, T) (wave69 Step 1: a multiple of the vector of "E smeared with f at time zero"; a smooth compactly supported TT field has finite Ḣ^{−3/2} norm because its Fourier transform vanishes at the origin). By finite propagation speed (T1) the curvature of k on D(B_{1+δ}) is that of h⁽¹⁾ = 2tK. If k̃ is another vector with that curvature on O, then (E, B)[k̃ − k] vanishes on B, so k̃ − k ∈ H(O)′, V(k̃ − k) ∈ R(H(O)′) ⊂ 𝒜(O)′, and ω_{k̃} = ω_k on 𝒜(O).

**M-b. The modular generator on k.**

*Lemma E (the flow acts geometrically wherever it is smooth).* Let G ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ⁴) be real and s₀ > 0 such that Λ_s is defined and smooth on a neighbourhood of supp G for |s| ≤ s₀. Then Δ_{H(O)}^{−is/2π} e_G = e_{Λ_{s*}G} for |s| ≤ s₀.
*Proof.* For F ∈ C_c^∞(O) the flow is defined on supp F for all s. wave70's Lemma 2(i) (finite covariance of the two-point function, on any open set where the flow is smooth) gives ⟨e_{Λ_{s*}G}, e_{Λ_{s*}F}⟩ = ⟨e_G, e_F⟩. By Theorem F the right member of the left pairing is Δ^{−is/2π}e_F, so ⟨e_{Λ_{s*}G}, Δ^{−is/2π}e_F⟩ = ⟨Δ^{−is/2π}e_G, Δ^{−is/2π}e_F⟩. The vectors Δ^{−is/2π}e_F = e_{Λ_{s*}F} run over all e_{F′}, F′ ∈ C_c^∞(O), whose complex span is dense (wave70 Lemma 3). ∎

*Lemma G (generator).* Every vector u of the form "E or B smeared at time zero with a field in C_c^∞(ℝ³)" lies in Dom(log Δ_{H(O)}), and the curvature of w = i log Δ_{H(O)} u is the distribution 2π δ_ζ C_u on ℝ⁴, where C_u is the curvature of u and δ_ζ the Lie action of ζ with weight 3. On the slice it is 2π times (1).
*Proof.* Let u be the vector of f ⊗ δ(t) and G_n = f ⊗ δ_n(t), δ_n(t) = nδ₁(nt) a mollifier. The supports lie in one compact set, so Lemma E applies with one s₀. s ↦ Λ_{s*}G_n is C¹ into C_c^∞ of a fixed compact set and G ↦ e_G is continuous there, so e_{G_n} ∈ Dom(log Δ) (Stone) with i log Δ e_{G_n} = −2π e_{δ_ζ†G_n}, δ_ζ† the transpose of δ_ζ. The wave function of e_G is a fixed matrix of size ω^{3/2} times the Fourier transform of G on the cone. δ_ζ†G_n is a finite sum of terms a(x)·t^mδ_n^{(q)}(t), a ∈ C_c^∞, m ≤ 2, q ≤ 1, whose Fourier transforms in t are bounded by C(1 + ω) uniformly in n and converge pointwise. By dominated convergence e_{G_n} → u and e_{δ†G_n} converges in ℋ. log Δ is closed, so u ∈ Dom(log Δ). For any real F ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ⁴), Lemma E applied to F gives C_{Δ^{it}u}(F) = C_u(Λ_{2πt*}F) for small t; the derivative at t = 0 is C_w(F) = 2π C_u(δ_ζ†F) = 2π(δ_ζC_u)(F). ∎

For our k the curvature near Ō is smooth and stationary: E = 0, B = −curl K, ∂_tE = curl curl K = −ΔK = 0, ∂_tB = 0. So by Lemma G, (1) and (K3), on the open ball

  (E, B)[i log Δ k] = 2π·((l+1)K, 0).  (2)

The sign is wave70's part (b) in the conventions of Section 1 (its B is −B here).

**M-c. The cut vector.** Put c = (l+1)/(l+2) and let h_v be the tensor field equal to 2cNK on B and 0 outside. It is Lipschitz with compact support, so it has finite Ḣ^{1/2} norm. Its linearised scalar curvature ∂_i∂_jh_ij − Δh_kk vanishes as a distribution: inside B it is −2c(tr K + x·div K) = 0, and since h_v vanishes on S the surface part is (−n·div(2cNK) + ∂_n tr)δ_S = 2c K_nn δ_S = 0 by (K2) (`s1` M5). A symmetric tensor field with zero linearised scalar curvature is its TT part plus sym∇ξ (T4). Let v ∈ ℋ be the vector (P_TT h_v, 0). Then

  E[v] = Ric₃(h_v), a distribution supported in B̄, equal to c(l+2)K = (l+1)K on B by (K4);  B[v] = 0.

So v ∈ N(B̄) = H(O) by Theorem D, and by (2) the curvature of i log Δ k − 2πv vanishes on B, so i log Δ k − 2πv ∈ H(O)′. Therefore

  P_{H(O)}(i log Δ k) = 2πv.

*H(O) is factorial,* so that P is well defined. If x ∈ H ∩ H′ then S_Hx = x and, by Proposition SQ (a), S_H*x = x, so Δ^{1/2}x = Δ^{−1/2}x and x is fixed by Δ^{it}. By wave70 Step 7, Δ_{H(O)}^{it} is unitarily equivalent to the group V(s) of boosts of a wedge, so it is enough that no non-zero vector is boost invariant. Let T(a) be the unitary translations along a null direction e₊ of the boost plane; V(s)T(a)V(−s) = T(e^{s}a) (wave70 Lemma 2). If V(s)y = y then ⟨y, T(a)y⟩ = ⟨y, T(e^{s}a)y⟩ for all s, so it is constant on a > 0 and on a < 0, hence by continuity equal to ‖y‖², hence T(a)y = y. The same holds for e₋, so y is invariant under time translations. Time translation multiplies the wave function by e^{iωt}, so y is supported on ω = 0, a null set, and y = 0.

**M-d. The value.** By I1,

  S(ω_k‖ω₀) = −Im⟨k, P_{H(O)} i log Δ k⟩ = −2π Im⟨k, v⟩ = −(2π/32πG)∫(h_k·p_v − h_v^{TT}·p_k).

p_v = 0. P_TT is self-adjoint in the L² pairing and p_k is TT, so ∫h_v^{TT}·p_k = ∫_B 2cN K·p_k. On B_{1+δ}, curl(p_k − K) = 0 with p_k − K symmetric, so p_k − K = Hess g with g smooth on B̄ (Poincaré lemma twice). Integrating by parts, using N = 0 on S, ∇N = −x, div K = 0, tr K = 0 and (K2):

  ∫_B N K·Hess g = ∫_B ∂_jg (K·x)_j = ∮_S g K_nn − ∫_B g (tr K + x·div K) = 0  (`s1` M6).

Hence

## 6. Proof of (C)

Let (M, Φ, ι) obey H1–H3 and k ∈ ℋ.
1. By H2 and Theorem D, Φ(M) ⊂ 𝒜(O′)′ = 𝒜(O).
2. *Support.* Let s ∈ M be the support projection of ω_k∘Φ. Φ(1 − s) is a positive element of 𝒜(O) with ω_k(Φ(1 − s)) = 0. V(k)Ω is cyclic and separating for 𝒜(O), because V(k)𝒜(O)V(k)* = 𝒜(O) and Ω is (Proposition SQ (b)). So Φ(1 − s) = 0, and by the Schwarz inequality Φ(m(1 − s)) = Φ((1 − s)m) = 0: Φ(m) = Φ(sms). (If ω_k∘Φ is faithful on M, then s = 1 and this step is empty.)
3. *Upper bound.* α = Φ restricted to sMs, into 𝒜(O), is unital and completely positive. The first states are vector states of cyclic vectors: V(k)Ω for 𝒜(O), and the representative of the faithful state ω_k∘Φ in the standard form of sMs. I3 gives S_{sMs}(ω_k∘Φ‖ω₀∘Φ) ≤ S_{𝒜(O)}(ω_k‖ω₀).

## 7. Proof of (B)

F[g; S, Σ] = Area_g(S) − 2Vol_g(Σ), for a spacelike 2-sphere S near ∂B and a spacelike slice Σ near B with ∂Σ = S. Write δA[q] = ½∮_S(q_kk − q_nn), δV[q] = ½∫_B q_kk, R_lin[q] = ∂_i∂_jq_ij − Δq_kk.

**B-a. Gauge.** Let ψ_ε be the flow of −εξ. ψ_ε*g_ε is again a family obeying H5, with first-order part 2tK, and F[ψ_ε*g_ε; S, Σ] = F[g_ε; ψ_ε(S), ψ_ε(Σ)]. So it suffices to take ξ = 0; configurations are then moved back by ψ_ε, a displacement of size O(ε).

**B-b. The coordinate ball.** h⁽¹⁾ = 2tK vanishes at t = 0, so the induced metric on {t = 0} is δ + ε²q₂ + O(ε³), q₂ the spatial part of h⁽²⁾ there, and F at the coordinate ball is F₀ + ε²(δA − 2δV)[q₂] + O(ε³). Green's identity with ∇∇N = −δ, N|_S = 0, ∇N|_S = −n gives, for every smooth symmetric q,

  ∫_B N R_lin[q] = −2δA[q] + 4δV[q]  (`s2` B1).

**B-c. The constraint.** The lapse is 1 + O(ε²) and the shift O(ε²), so the extrinsic curvature of {t = 0} is εK + O(ε²). The scalar curvature of δ + ε²q₂ is ε²R_lin[q₂] + O(ε⁴) (`s2` B3). The Hamiltonian constraint R + (tr k)² − k_ijk^ij = 0 at O(ε²) reads

  R_lin[q₂] = |K|².

So F(coordinate ball) = F₀ − (ε²/2)A + O(ε³), whatever h⁽²⁾ is (`s2` B4: three second-order gauges, three harmonics, l = 2, 3, 4; −2δA alone moves with the gauge, the total does not).

**B-d. Displaced configurations.** Displace S to r = 1 + σ, t = τ, and Σ to t = s with s|_S = τ. To second order in ε and in the displacement d = (σ, τ, s),

  F = F₀ − (ε²/2)A + ε L_K(d) + Q(d) + O(ε²|d| + ε|d|² + |d|³).

- *No drive.* The metric perturbation 2εtK vanishes at t = 0, so σ does not enter at order ε, and L_K(d) = ∮_S τ(tr K − K_nn) − 2∫_B s tr K. Both integrands vanish by (K1), (K2). **L_K ≡ 0 on the scope family.**
- *The quadratic form* is flat space's: Q = ∮(½|∇_Sσ|² − σ²) − ½∮|∇_Sτ|² + ∫_B|∇s|² (`s2` B6, from the area and volume elements). After minimising over s at fixed τ it is ½(j−1)(j+2)∮σ_j² − ½j(j−1)∮τ_j² on degree j. It is nondegenerate except on σ_{j=1}, τ_{j=0}, τ_{j=1}: the seven Poincaré motions of the ball that are not rotations.
- *Consequence.* The gradient of F at the coordinate ball is O(ε²). Let d_ε = O(ε) be a configuration whose gradient is o(ε). Split d = d^⊥ + (Poincaré part). In the nondegenerate directions the gradient is DQ(d^⊥) + O(ε²), so d^⊥ = o(ε) and Q(d^⊥) = o(ε²). A Poincaré motion P_a with a = O(ε) can be undone by pulling the metric back; P_a*g_ε is a family of the same kind whose first-order datum differs from 2tK by O(ε), which changes F at O(ε³). Every remaining term is O(ε³). So F(d_ε) = F₀ − (ε²/2)A + o(ε²).

**B-e. The Poincaré modes are not driven.** The ε²-coefficient of F at the ball moved by P_a is, by B-b and B-c applied to P_a*g_ε, a quadratic functional 𝒬 of the first-order solution alone, invariant under the symmetries of (η, B). Its derivative along a generator X is 2𝒬(h⁽¹⁾, £_Xh⁽¹⁾). Under the inversion x → −x, h⁽¹⁾ is an eigenvector (single l) and a translation generator is odd, so the derivative along translations vanishes. Under t → −t, h⁽¹⁾ = 2tK is odd and the generators of time translation and of boosts are odd, so £_Xh⁽¹⁾ is even and the derivative vanishes. So the coordinate ball is stationary through O(ε²) in all directions (wave69/bclose measured ≤ 3e−8 per unit ε² on five members). ∎

**Numerical confirmation, not part of the proof** (stored, wave69/bclose `out/p2_geo.json`): on exact polynomial solutions with all ten components of the nonlinear Ricci tensor zero through O(ε²), the extremum of A − 2V located numerically at ten values of ε gives Δ₂/A_l = 1 within 5e−10 on six members, one with a time-odd term and a second-order gauge term.

**The corner modifier, closed.** wave69/bclose found that the Noether charge of a fixed ζ evaluated on a moved corner is −6δA + 14δV, an effective k = 7/3. It is not a functional of the geometry: on a pure-gauge 10% dilation it is 4π/5, not zero, and it changes with the second-order gauge on shell (`s2`, two plants). d(A − kV) is invariant under q → q + sym∇ξ with ξ free on S only at k = 2, the mean curvature of S (`s2` B2). ## 11. The referee attack, and the answers

I attacked the theorem as the most hostile referee I could play. Thirteen attacks; three changed the text.

1. **"The direct proof needs k in the domain of log Δ, and k is not localised in the ball."** This broke my first draft, which quoted wave70's part (b) for vectors in the domain without showing k is in it. Fixed by Lemmas E and G: the flow acts geometrically on every e_G for short times, by covariance of the two-point function and Reeh–Schlieder; closedness of log Δ carries it to time-zero vectors.
2. **"Uhlmann's theorem as printed needs the first state to be a vector state of a cyclic vector. On an abstract M it need not be."** This broke the two-line proof of (C) in cderive and Paper A. Fixed by step 2 of Section 6: the support projection s, Φ(m) = Φ(sms), and both bounds on sMs. No new hypothesis.
3. **"Nobody on file proves R(K)′ = R(K′) or Δ = Γ(Δ_K)."** True, and both routes to (M) and the route to (C) use them. Fixed by Proposition SQ. What remains imported is Tomita–Takesaki and Takesaki's theorem, named T6 and T7, with locators from memory.
4. **"The stationary point of A − 2V is not unique: seven zero modes."** Answered by B-d, B-e. The theorem claims the value, not uniqueness of the configuration.
5. **"At a fixed coordinate ball the second-order area side is gauge dependent; Paper A's own Appendix D says so."** Yes, for data with K_nn ≠ 0 (electric: `s1` plant P4, where the cut vector also breaks the constraint). On the scope family L_K ≡ 0, so the coordinate ball in the gauge 2tK is the stationary one. In another first-order gauge the stationary configuration is the moved ball (B-a), and the theorem says so.
6. **"The cut vector's curvature has a delta layer on S. Is it in the ball algebra?"** H(O) = N(B̄) allows support on the closed ball (Theorem D, which is where Lemma A's dilation is used). The comparison (2) is on the open ball, which is exactly H(O)′ = N(ℝ³∖B).
7. **"p_k is not K: TT potentials are non-local."** Right. p_k − K = Hess g on the ball, and the pairing with NK kills every Hessian (M6). An electric K fails this check.
8. **"The normalisation 1/8G could be off by 2: Brunetti–Guido–Longo's Weyl convention halves the entropy."** The convention is fixed in Section 1 as CLR's, with Im = half the canonical symplectic form; the scalar gives S = 2π∫N T₀₀ in the same convention. 9. **"(C) assumes its conclusion: A3 from inside, A2 from outside."** (C) is the statement that for the ball nothing lies between the interior algebra and the exterior's commutant. That is duality, a theorem with content (false for a ring). Whether the physical algebra is a channel image at all is H1, a hypothesis.
10. **"The comparison is between an exact quantity and a second-order one."** Stated in (L): the ladder is the O(ε²) statement. The modular side happens to have no higher order.
11. **"H4 picks k = 2 and extremality by hand."** k = 2 is forced by invariance under corner diffeomorphisms (`s2` B2). Extremality against fixed volume: at this order they agree when L_K = 0. Proposition R reduces H4 to the first-order first law plus four premises. H4 remains a hypothesis.
12. **"The two routes to (M) are not independent: same author family, same conventions."** They share I1, SQ and the definition of the net, and are otherwise different mathematics. They were not written blind to each other. This is listed in Section 12.
13. **"Is the factor (l+1) an artefact of the weight-3 action?"** wave70 S1: weights 2 and 4 and the opposite boost sign each fail invariance of the two-point function in 276 to 304 of 324 components. Exactly one action is a symmetry.
