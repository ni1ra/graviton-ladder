**Bin: PROOF** (on the strictest reading of the PREREG wording, PROOF-WITH-CONDITION, the condition being Araki's abstract duality until its printed proof is read line by line). A(O)′ = A(O′) for every double cone O. The proof is direct and elementary: it uses neither Hislop 1988 nor conformal covariance. Its one imported theorem is Araki's abstract duality R(K)′ = R(K′) for second quantisation, whose statement I read in two files and whose printed proof (Garbarz–Palau §5.3) I read in outline, not line by line. ## 1. The theorem

**Setting.**
- **One-particle space.** H is the space of pairs u = (h, π) of transverse traceless (TT) symmetric tensor fields on ℝ³, completed in ‖h‖²_{Ḣ^{1/2}} + ‖π‖²_{Ḣ^{-1/2}}, with complex structure J(h, π) = (ω⁻¹π, −ωh), ω = |∇|, and σ(u, u′) = Im⟨u, u′⟩ = ∫(h·π′ − h′·π). Time evolution is ḣ = π, π̇ = Δh. This is linearised gravity in TT gauge: helicity +2 plus helicity −2.
- **Curvature.** E[u] = −½Δh and B[u] = ½ curl π, with (curl T)_ij = ε_ikl ∂_k T_lj. Both are TT, Ė = curl B, Ḃ = −curl E, and u ↦ (E[u], B[u]) is a real-linear bijection of H onto Ḣ^{-3/2}_TT ⊕ Ḣ^{-3/2}_TT with ‖u‖² = 4(‖E‖² + ‖B‖²). On shell every component of the linearised Riemann (Weyl) tensor is a component of E or B at that time.
- **Net.** W(u) are the Weyl operators on the Fock space over H. For a double cone O, K(O) ⊂ H is the closed real span of the vectors of curvature smeared with test tensors supported in O, and A(O) = R(K(O)) = {W(u) : u ∈ K(O)}″. A(O′) is generated by the A(O₁), O₁ a double cone spacelike to O.

**Theorem.** For every double cone O = D(B), B an open ball in a time slice,

  A(O)′ = A(O′).

**Corollaries proved on the way.**
- **C1 (what the ball algebra is).** K(O) = N(B̄): the ball's one-particle space is exactly the set of finite-energy data whose E and B vanish outside the closed ball. Nothing more, nothing less.
- **C2 (outer regularity).** ∩_{δ>0} A(D_{r+δ}) = A(D_r).
- **C3.** Duality also holds from the other side: A(O′)′ = A(O).

## 2. The proof

Notation: S²₀ = symmetric traceless 3×3 tensors. Constants below are exact (S1).

**Step 0. The Cotton–York operator.** For symmetric f put inc(f)_ij = ε_ikl ε_jmn ∂_k∂_m f_ln and

  C f := curl(inc f − ½ δ tr inc f).

C is a third-order constant-coefficient operator. For every symmetric f: C f is symmetric, traceless and divergence-free; C(∂_(i v_j)) = 0 and C(ψδ) = 0; C is formally self-adjoint; and curl Δ P_TT f = −C f. (S1 a–d, exact.) So C f is defined by local derivatives of f, although P_TT is not local.

**Step 1. The vectors of E(f) and B(g).** For f, g ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ³; S²₀) the functionals u ↦ ∫f·E[u] and u ↦ ∫g·B[u] are continuous on H, and

  σ(u, v_f) = ∫ f·E[u], with v_f = (0, −½ΔP_TT f), so E[v_f] = 0, B[v_f] = ¼ C f;
  σ(u, w_g) = ∫ g·B[u], with w_g = (−½ curl P_TT g, 0), so E[w_g] = −¼ C g, B[w_g] = 0.

Proof: integrate by parts; curl is symmetric on TT tensors; use Step 0. Both vectors have finite norm because their Fourier transforms carry |k|³ against the weight |k|⁻³. ∎

**Definitions.** For open U ⊂ ℝ³, L(U) := closed real span of {v_f, w_g : f, g ∈ C_c^∞(U; S²₀)}. For closed F, N(F) := {u ∈ H : supp E[u] ∪ supp B[u] ⊂ F}. (E[u] is a distribution: its pairing with a test tensor is the continuous functional of Step 1.)

**Step 2. L(U)′ = N(ℝ³∖U).** By Step 1, u is σ-orthogonal to every v_f and w_g with f, g supported in U exactly when E[u] and B[u] vanish as distributions on U. ∎ (This is where the symplectic complement becomes a support condition. It needs no topology.)

**Step 3. Lemma A (analysis; uses that B is a ball).** N(B̄_r) is the closure of {u : E[u], B[u] ∈ C_c^∞(B_r)}.

Proof. Take u ∈ N(B̄_r), centre at the origin.
- Dilate: u_λ with (E, B)_λ(x) = (E, B)(x/λ), λ < 1. Dilations act on Ḣ^{-3/2}_TT ⊕ Ḣ^{-3/2}_TT as a constant times a strongly continuous unitary group, and preserve TT. So u_λ → u as λ → 1, and supp ⊂ B̄_{λr}.
- Mollify: convolve with ρ_ε, ε < (1−λ)r. Convolution commutes with derivatives (TT is kept), has Fourier multiplier |ρ̂(εk)| ≤ 1 tending to 1 pointwise (so ρ_ε * u_λ → u_λ in H by dominated convergence), and gives smooth fields supported in B_r.
- A smooth compactly supported TT field T is in Ḣ^{-3/2}: k_i T̂_ij(k) = 0 for all k forces T̂(0) = 0, so |T̂|² = O(k²) at the origin. ∎

**Step 4. Lemma B (topology; uses that B is a ball).** If T is a smooth TT field with compact support in the open ball B, then T = C f for some f ∈ C_c^∞(B; S²₀).

Proof. The only analytic input is:

 (P) If w ∈ C_c^∞(B; ℝ³) has div w = 0, then w = curl a with a ∈ C_c^∞(B; ℝ³).

Proof of (P). Put a₀ = curl(G * w), G = 1/(4π|x|). Then curl a₀ = ∇ div(G*w) − Δ(G*w) = w. Let supp w ⊂ B̄_ρ, ρ < r. On {|x| > ρ}, curl a₀ = 0, and that region is simply connected, so a₀ = ∇χ there. With θ a cutoff equal to 0 near B̄_ρ and to 1 outside B_{ρ₂}, ρ < ρ₂ < r, take a = a₀ − ∇(θχ). ∎

Now the chain (every identity is S1 e1–e5 and e3b, exact):
1. For each j the vector T_{·j} is divergence-free. By (P), T_ij = ε_ikl ∂_k A_lj with A compactly supported in B.
2. tr T = 0 says that a_k := ε_kli A_li is divergence-free. By (P), a = curl v. Replace A by A − ∂_l v_j: the curl is unchanged and A is now symmetric.
3. T symmetric then says ∂_j A_pj − ∂_p tr A = 0: the tensor G := A − δ tr A is symmetric and divergence-free.
4. Apply (P) to G twice, as in 1 and 3: G = curl M, and symmetry of G makes M − δ tr M divergence-free, so it is ε_jmn ∂_m φ_pn. Then G = inc(φ^sym) exactly; the antisymmetric part of φ drops out.
5. A = G − ½ δ tr G, so T = curl(inc φ^sym − ½ δ tr inc φ^sym) = C φ^sym. Since C(ψδ) = 0, take f = the traceless part of φ^sym. All potentials are compactly supported in B. ∎

*Where the ball enters.* (P) was used four times, each time as "H²_c(B) = 0". For a general open U the same chain shows that the obstruction to T = C f with f supported in U lives in H²_c(U) ⊗ ℝ¹⁰ ≅ H₁(U) ⊗ {conformal Killing vectors}: the fluxes of T_ij ξ_j through surfaces spanning the non-contractible loops (S1 f; Benedetti–Casini–Magán eqs. 3.3–3.7). For E and B together that is their ℝ²⁰ per loop.

**Step 5. N(B̄) = L(B).** ⊃: v_f and w_g with f, g supported in B have (E, B) = (0, ¼Cf) and (−¼Cg, 0), supported in B, and N(B̄) is closed. ⊂: by Lemma A an element of N(B̄) is a limit of u_n with smooth TT (E_n, B_n) compactly supported in B; by Lemma B, E_n = C g_n and B_n = C f_n with f_n, g_n ∈ C_c^∞(B; S²₀); so u_n = 4v_{f_n} − 4w_{g_n} ∈ L(B). ∎

**Step 6. One-particle duality.** Let ext = ℝ³∖B̄. By Steps 2 and 5,

  L(ext)′ = N(B̄) = L(B),  hence  L(B)′ = L(ext)″ = L(ext).

**Step 7. From time zero to the double cone.** Let O = D(B_r), base at t = 0.
- **K(O) = L(B).** ⊃: E(f) and B(g), f, g ∈ C_c^∞(B), are limits of curvature smeared with f ⊗ δ_n(t), by strong continuity of the time evolution. ⊂: a curvature smearing in O is ∫dt [E_t(f_t) + B_t(g_t)] with (t, supp f_t), (t, supp g_t) ⊂ O. The vector of E_t(f_t) is v_{f_t} evolved by −t. Its (E, B) solve the wave equation (Ë = −curl² E = ΔE on TT) with smooth data supported in supp f_t, so at time zero they are supported within distance |t| of supp f_t, inside B. That is a smooth element of N(B̄), hence of L(B) by Lemma B. L(B) is closed, so the time integral stays in it.
- **K(O₁) ⊂ L(B)′ for every double cone O₁ spacelike to O.** Same argument: the time-zero (E, B) of a smearing in O₁ are supported at points x with |x| > r, so the vector lies in N(ℝ³∖B) = L(B)′ (Step 2). No boost and no covariance is needed.
- **L(ext) ⊂ K(O′).** A test tensor supported in ext is a finite sum of test tensors supported in small balls B_α with closure in ext (partition of unity; multiplying by a scalar keeps S²₀). Each v_{f_α} lies in L(B_α) = K(D(B_α)), and D(B_α) is spacelike to O.

So L(ext) ⊂ K(O′) ⊂ L(B)′ = L(ext), i.e. **K(O′) = K(O)′**. By Poincaré covariance of the construction the same holds for every double cone.

**Step 8. Second quantisation.** Araki's theorem: for closed real subspaces, ∨_α R(V_α) = R(∨_α V_α), ∧_α R(V_α) = R(∧_α V_α) and R(V)′ = R(V′). Hence

  A(O′) = ∨ R(K(O₁)) = R(K(O′)) = R(K(O)′) = R(K(O))′ = A(O)′. ∎

C1 is Steps 5 and 7. C2: ∩_δ L(B_{r+δ}) = ∩_δ N(B̄_{r+δ}) = N(B̄_r) = L(B_r), then Araki's ∧. C3 is the bicommutant.

## 3. The conformal route, and what "fails in general" means

- **What Lledó gives.** Prop. 5.8 (pdf p25): the helicity-n/2 nets are isotonous, causal, additive and covariant under SU(2,2). The list after it (pdf p26) claims essential duality and timelike duality for light cones. Double-cone duality is not claimed.
- **What Brunetti–Guido–Longo give.** Duality R(O)′ = R(O^c) holds on the cover M̃, where O^c is the causal complement there. Their worked example (paper pp17–18, "Examples derived from free fields") is the scalar: for even d the theory lives on the compactification M̄, O^c is the spacelike complement O′ together with the timelike complement O^t, and Haag duality on Minkowski space holds because of the additional fact R(O′) = R(O^c), which they take from Hislop–Longo.
- **So the conformal route is not a proof by itself.** Covariance plus wedge duality gives R(O)′ = R(O^c). To reach Minkowski duality one must still show that the algebra of the far timelike cones, and of the points at infinity, is already generated in the spacelike complement. That is a statement about Cauchy data in the exterior of the ball: the same content as Steps 2–7.
- **"Fails in general"** refers to nets that live on a proper cover or carry extra structure: their examples are the restriction to a time axis for d > 2 and the tensor product A₄ ⊗ A₄ on ℝ² (p19). It is not a statement about integer-helicity free nets in d = 4.
- **Consequence of the theorem, read backwards.** With R(O)′ = R(O′) proved, the timelike-cone algebras lie in A(O′) whenever they commute with A(O) (Huygens' principle for the curvature commutator). I did not need this and did not check it.
- **Hislop 1988.** A web search returned the abstract only (University of Kentucky and OSTI records): the modular conjugations are used "to establish duality for double-cone algebras" for free massless models of arbitrary helicity. The present proof agrees with that claim and is independent of it. I have not read the paper.

## 5. What this means for clause C, clause M and Paper A's citation

- **Input (H) of the C theorem now stands on a proof we hold.** wave64/cderive Theorem 1 uses A(D′)′ = A(D) with A(D′) generated by the A(O), O ⊂ D′. That is the theorem above, for the same net (the Weyl closure of smeared curvature). cderive's weakest step 3 ("that Hislop's net is the record's net is not checked") no longer applies: no identification with Hislop's or Lledó's net is used.
- **"Nothing hides on the edge" is sharper than cderive stated it.** C1 says the commutant of the exterior is generated by curvature smeared strictly inside. Any operator on the Fock space that commutes with every exterior curvature observable is in A(D). C2 gives the ∩_δ statement (slice s1b) directly, without w41-outerreg's dilation argument, though by the same mechanism (a dilation, here in Lemma A).
- **The remark in cderive §3 is confirmed and corrected in one place.** The obstruction is H₁ of the region, not π₂: Lemma B's obstruction group is H²_c(U) ≅ H₁(U), which vanishes for the ball and for a spherical shell. The sphere-flux argument in cderive §3 (S1 there) is the same fact seen from outside. cderive's phrase "Brunetti–Guido–Longo prove it for conformal nets in general" is wrong as written: they prove it on the cover, and on Minkowski space only with an extra input (§3).
- **Clause M.** Whatever in Paper A's core theorem used double-cone duality for the curvature net may cite this proof. I did not re-read the paper's use of it (fence), so I cannot say whether M uses duality in exactly this form; the form proved is A(O)′ = A(O′) with A(O′) additive over double cones.
- **Suggested citation for Paper A.** "Haag duality for double cones of the free helicity-2 curvature net: Hislop, Ann. Phys. 185 (1988) 193; an elementary proof in the style of Araki and Leyland–Roberts–Testard is given in Appendix X." The appendix is §2 of this file. Do not cite Brunetti–Guido–Longo for Minkowski double-cone duality.
- **What the theorem does not touch.** It is a statement about the G⁰ free curvature net. The definitional slices of cderive (s3, s4o_q, s5d: a chosen edge state, a quantum clock, dressing at G > 0) are axiom failures, not duality failures, and stand where they stood. A net that also contains the metric potential, or regions with non-contractible loops, is outside the theorem; for rings duality does fail, by ℝ²⁰ per loop.
