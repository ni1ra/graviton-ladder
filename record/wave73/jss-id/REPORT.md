## 2. JSS's definitions as written (arXiv:2306.01837v3, printed page numbers) [S1]

- **A3/A4, p. 16.** H^g_xi is "the generator of a specific boost-like flow"; "The flow generated by H^g_xi coincides with the modular flow for some state". The state is a vector in H_QFT (eq. 2.6), so a normal state.
- **(2.3) and text, p. 11.** H^g_xi generates "the flow of xi^a ... instantaneously on Sigma_c" and is "a local integral of the matter and graviton stress tensors".
- **(3.20) to (3.25), pp. 23–24.** With g = g0 + kappa·h, the residual current is kappa(4G^(2)_ab − T^(0)_ab)xi^a; C^(1)[xi] = (H^g)^(1) + H_obs^(0) + H_ADM^(0).
- **(3.26) to (3.28), p. 24.** {h, H^g_{kappa xi}} = L_xi g0 + kappa·L_xi h, hence {h, (H^g)^(1)} = L_xi h. (H^g)^(0) "generates the linearized diffeomorphism" L_xi g0. Footnote 8 flags "a small subtlety".
- **(3.11) to (3.13), pp. 20–21.** H^g = H^Sigma − H^Sigma-bar; the constraint integral over Sigma is H^Sigma + H_obs + (kappa_s/2pi)·A/4G. The area terms cancel in the two-sided sum.
- **Section 3.3, pp. 26–28.** Gauge condition (H^Sigma)^(−1) = 0, for a diamond "proportional to the first order change in the volume". At second order, a proposal only: extremise kappa_s·A/8piG + V[xi], where V[xi] has first variation equal to (H^Sigma)^(−1). "We do not treat this problem in detail".
- **Section 4.3, p. 31.** "the graviton part of H^g_xi is a known quadratic 'Hamiltonian' for a free spin-2 field. We relegate it to future work". The CFT ball is the vacuum by Hislop–Longo.

## 4. What our theorem says under each reading

- **(i) Area side, fixed volume:** A4 fails for odd gravitons on a flat ball in the vacuum sector (given the hinge theorem [I]).
- **(ii) Covariant flow on curvature:** A4 holds with the vacuum. The theorem then says the fixed-volume area term is not the modular energy: they differ by Z = ½c_l∫psi², so (2.13) with Section 3.3's surface fails at this order and no normal state repairs it [B, hand argument].

- **(iv) Other rescaling weights c:** the charge is c_l·E_2 + ((2 − c)/4)∫|h|²; only c = 2 is gauge invariant, which is (i) again.
