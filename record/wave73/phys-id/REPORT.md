**PHYS-ID report.** Items 1b, 2 and 3 are covered by sources I opened. Item 1 is covered but no longer needed. Item 4 is covered only by chaining three papers, and one condition has to be stated. For items 5 and 6 I found nothing in print that already states our result.

**1. KMS implies modular (no longer needed if 1b stands).** Source: Hiai, *Concise lectures on von Neumann algebras*, [arXiv:2004.02383](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.02383v1), Thm 2.14 and Def. 2.11, citing Takesaki. Statement: take M with a faithful normal positive functional ω. Then σ^ω is the only weakly continuous one-parameter automorphism group for which ω is (α, β = −1)-KMS. The strip is 0 < Im z < 1, with F(t) = ω(α_t(x)y) and F(t+i) = ω(yα_t(x)). Faithfulness has to be supplied; Dereziński (p. 43) notes that a KMS state on a factor is automatically faithful. Weak continuity gives strong* continuity. **COVERED.**

**1b. Modular data of a second-quantised algebra.** Source: Dereziński, [math-ph/0511030](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0511030), v2 PDF, Thm 40, §9.3, pp. 40–44, read first-hand.
- Setting: Fock space over Z ⊕ Z̄, with 0 ≤ γ ≤ 1, Ker(γ−1) = {0} and ρ = γ(1−γ)⁻¹. The left algebra is generated by W((1+ρ)^½ z, ρ̄^½ z̄) for z in Dom ρ^½. This is exactly the referee's form, with c = (1+ρ)^½ and s = ρ^½.
- Statement (6): Ω is cyclic iff it is separating iff Ker γ = {0}. Then J = Γ(ε) and Δ = Γ(γ ⊕ γ̄⁻¹).
- With γ = e^{−2πh}: Ker γ = {0} holds automatically, and Ker(γ−1) = {0} means Ker h = {0}, so no zero mode. The two-point function ½(z|(1+2ρ)z) gives exactly the weight coth(πh).
- The general real-subspace version is reduced to Thm 40 inside the proof of Thm 54 (§12.3, p. 65, via Thm 53(2)), which needs "general position". Neeb, [arXiv:2511.09360](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09360v1) §1.7 Lemma 9(v)–(vi), gives: Ω is cyclic iff V + iV is dense, and separating iff V̄ ∩ iV̄ = {0}.
- I did not find the explicit formula Δ = Γ(Δ_V) for a general standard V stated in a theorem.

**COVERED** for the referee's direct construction. The paper should cite Thm 40(6) and state Ker h = {0}.

**2. GNS of a quasi-free state.** Source: Dereziński Thm 41, §9.4, p. 45, first-hand. Hypotheses: η is nondegenerate and ω is nondegenerate on the completion. Conclusion: an *isometry* U into the Araki–Woods Fock space with UΨ = Ω. U is unitary because Ω is cyclic (Thm 40(6)). His normalisation is e^{−¼yηy}, not Kay–Wald's. **COVERED WITH A CONDITION**, and unnecessary once 1b is used.

**3. Totality and continuity.** Totality: Neeb §1.7. The lemma prints as "Lemma 8" in the HTML and "1.28" in search excerpts, so check the number in the PDF. It holds on any complex Hilbert space, and W(k)Ω = e^{−‖k‖²/4} Exp(ik/√2) by my own derivation. Continuity: Dereziński p. 65 ("strong continuity of w ↦ W(w)") and Thm 40(1) (regular representation). **COVERED.**

**4. Ball modular group.**
- Hislop–Longo 1982: massless scalar, D = 4. Hislop 1988, Ann. Phys. 185, 193: I read only the [OSTI abstract](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/6828567). It says "arbitrary helicity": the Poincaré representation extends to SU(2,2), the algebras of double cones are generated by "standard" linear fields, and the modular groups are one-parameter conformal groups. Helicity 2 is not named.
- Lledó, Rev. Math. Phys. 13 (2001), [math-ph/0006018](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0006018): free nets are conformally covariant for any helicity. They are built from D(0,n/2) ⊕ D(n/2,0) test functions, and n = 4 is the Weyl spinor. I did not read his §5.
- Brunetti–Guido–Longo, [funct-an/9302008](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/funct-an/9302008), Thm 2.3(ii): for a local net covariant under the cover of the conformal group, with positive energy and a vacuum, Δ^{it} = U(Λ_t). Here Λ_t is the conformal flow preserving the double cone, and the wedge case has cosh 2πt, which matches our 2πt.
- No paper names linearised gravity. **COVERED WITH A CONDITION:** the curvature algebra of the ball must equal the helicity ±2 free-net algebra of the double cone, and its conformal covariance must hold in BGL's sense. The flow is geometric on Ψ_ABCD including its conformal weight factor.
