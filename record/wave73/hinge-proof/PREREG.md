## Statement under test

(T) d = 4, flat unit ball B, free helicity-2 net, M = R(H) the ball algebra in the vacuum
representation. Let T_t be the symplectic flow on H generated by the area-side charge, which on each
odd (l, m) sector, l >= 2, is (record input) the flow of 2 pi E_2 = K_Omega + 2 pi Z,
Z = (g/2) int_B phi^2, g = 1. Claim: there is no faithful normal semifinite weight (in particular
no faithful normal state) on M whose modular group is V(h) -> V(T_t h).

## The argument as numbered steps, and what would count as failure

1. Reduction (record input). Each odd (l, m) graviton sector is unitarily a scalar l-wave sector;
   vacuum modular generator 2 pi E_1, area-side charge 2 pi E_2.
   FAILS IF: the record does not prove this for all odd-parity data, or the flow is not block
   diagonal in (l, m, parity). I can only check what the record claims, not re-prove it.
2. Frame identification. Vacuum on the ball = beta = 2 pi thermal state of the conformally coupled
   field on R x H^3; Z becomes the potential g N(rho), N = 1/(1 + cosh rho); sector operator
   h_l^2 = -d^2/drho^2 + lambda^2/sinh^2(rho), lambda^2 = l(l+1).
   FAILS IF: the radial operator or the potential is wrong (I re-derive it), or the theorem cited
   (Hislop-Longo; Casini-Huerta-Myers map) does not cover the scalar sector.
3. The flow exists on H: T_t maps H to H boundedly. (Not addressed by HINGE.)
   FAILS IF: the generator perturbation is unbounded in the one-particle norm on H.
4. Criterion (HINGE Prop. 1): weight exists => S_t (+) 1, S_t = T_t Delta^{-it}, extends to a
   bounded symplectic map with Hilbert-Schmidt antilinear part.
   FAILS IF: Connes' cocycle theorem does not apply to weights; the implementer is not in M; the
   density/boundedness step is wrong; Shale's theorem is used in the wrong direction or without
   invertibility.
5. First-order sector formula for ||beta_t^(l)||^2 (thermofield weights).
   FAILS IF: an independent exact computation (my own code) disagrees with HINGE's table.
6. Scaling limit lambda^2 ||beta^(l)||^2 -> g^2 J(t)/2 > 0, and a_1 = 7 zeta(3)/(16 pi^2).
   FAILS IF: the limit is zero or the approach is not from a positive constant. (Number already
   supported by hinge-blind; I do not redo it.)
7. Uniformity: the exact ||beta_t^(l)||^2_HS >= c/lambda^2 for all large l, c > 0.
   FAILS IF: the remainder beyond first order is not o(1/lambda) in a norm strong enough to give a
   lower bound. This is HINGE's named gap. Plan: prove an operator-norm bound on the generator
   perturbation, ||D_l|| <= C|g|/lambda, from the pointwise inequality N^2 <= 1/sinh^2(rho), then a
   vector-wise Duhamel bound (a lower bound on a Hilbert-Schmidt norm needs only one unit vector per
   sector, so operator-norm control of the remainder suffices).
8. Sum over sectors: sum_{l,m} ||beta^(l,m)||^2 = infinity (multiplicity 2l+1), so no weight.
   FAILS IF: blocks are not orthogonal invariant subspaces of the full one-particle space.
9. Electric/even tower "can only add". FAILS IF: the charge is not defined or not block diagonal there.
10. Reference/gauge independence. FAILS IF: the conclusion depends on using the vacuum as reference,
    or an admissible change of the charge (edge term, lapse) removes the divergence.
11. State corollary via product states (Kakutani type). FAILS IF: type III product criterion
    misapplied. (Redundant with 4 if 4 holds.)
12. Identification with JSS's assumption A4 (their H^g_xi is this charge). Cannot be tested here.

## Numerical test of step 7 (smallest test that could falsify uniformity)

Script `scripts/u1_uniformity.py`, my own code, float64, grid rho in (0, 50], h = 0.04
(n = 1250), Dirichlet at 0, Neumann outer wall, t = 0.5, l in {4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128}, g = +1, -1.
Exact (all orders in g) quantities in the discretised sector:

- U1: mu_l = lambda * ||N h_l^{-1}||_op. PASS iff mu_l <= 1 + 1e-9 for every l.
  (The discrete model obeys the same inequality, so a violation means my lemma or code is wrong.)
- U2: nu_l = ||S_t - 1|| in the one-particle norm on H. PASS iff
  nu_l <= exp(2 sqrt(2) pi |g| t / lambda) - 1 for every l and both signs.
- U3: r_l = ||S_t(g) - 1 - g B1||, B1 by central difference at g0 = 1/64. PASS iff
  lambda^2 r_l <= 4 pi^2 g^2 t^2 exp(2 sqrt(2) pi |g| t/lambda) * 1.02 (2% slack for the finite
  difference) for every l and both signs.
- U4: E_l(g) = exact ||beta_t^(l)||^2_HS; E1_l = first-order coefficient (mean of g = +-1/64,
  divided by g0^2); dev_l(g) = E_l(g)/E1_l - 1 (g = +-1).
  PASS iff |dev_128(g)| <= 0.25 |dev_16(g)| for both signs AND lambda^2 E1_128 >= lambda^2 E1_16 > 0.
  FAIL (uniformity falsified or unsupported) otherwise.
- U5 (cross-code check of step 5): l^2 E-type numbers vs HINGE's table row "l^2 ||beta_l(0.5)||^2"
  (0.244 at l = 16, 0.278 at l = 64). PASS iff my l^2 * mean_{+-1} E_l is within 3% of each.
- C1 (control, step 4 of the brief): same computation with the lapse-weighted potential g N^2
  (mass-type term, for which local normality is the published expectation: Eckmann-Frohlich 1974
  for d = 4; JSS Sec. 4.3). PASS iff l^2 E_l(N^2) at l = 64 is <= 0.5 times its value at l = 16,
  while for the sharp potential N the same ratio is >= 1. If the lapse-weighted control also
  plateaus, the method cannot tell existence from non-existence and step 8 is void.

Expected before running: all pass; dev ratio about 0.13 to 0.2.

## Counterexample search (analytic, decided before running)

Apply the same reasoning to: (a) Killing case; (b) temperature rescaling of the vacuum flow
(non-existence known exactly: outer modular automorphisms of a type III_1 factor); (c) mass term /
lapse-weighted term in d = 4 (existence expected); (d) a 2d thermal interval (normal state exists,
lapse differs at second order at the edge); (e) the null-local theorem of 2607.20678.
The argument is BROKEN if it yields non-existence in (c) or (d), or existence in (b).

## What would change my mind

A violation of U1 to U3 (my lemma is wrong); a dev_l that does not shrink; a control that plateaus;
a reading of the record showing the reduction is only for stationary data; a solvable case in which
the reasoning gives the wrong answer.
