Entropy cannot tell electric tides from magnetic ones. Area can.

An exact second-order mismatch for free gravitons in a flat ball

Andreas Houg. Nightly preview after version 3, 10 November 2026. Not peer reviewed. Version 4 in preparation.

Put a weak tidal gravitational field through a ball in flat space. Two quantities respond at second order in the field: the relative entropy of the gravitons inside the ball, and the area side, the drop in the stationary value of Area − 2·Volume divided by 4G (an area deficit at fixed volume). The first law of causal diamonds, carried to second order, would make them equal. We computed both exactly in free linearised gravity. For static electric tides they agree. For stationary magnetic tides the entropy is only (l+1)/(l+2) of the area side, where l is the multipole order of the tide.

What was computed: graviton relative entropy against the area side

Take a ball in flat space and its causal diamond, the piece of spacetime that an observer confined to the ball can influence and observe. Put a weak vacuum tidal field of amplitude ε through it. Two numbers, both of order ε², are compared.

  • The entropy side. Quantise linearised gravity. A tidal field is a coherent state of gravitons. Its relative entropy to the vacuum, both restricted to the observables in the diamond, measures how well an observer confined to the diamond can tell the tidal field from empty space. For a coherent state the von Neumann entropy of the ball does not change, so this relative entropy is the change in the expectation of the modular Hamiltonian of the ball. It is not the entanglement entropy of gravitons in the vacuum, which is a different quantity (see Benedetti and Casini, arXiv:1908.01800).
  • The area side. For a closed surface near the unit sphere and a slice it bounds, take F = Area − 2·Volume; the 2 is the mean curvature of the unit sphere. The unit ball makes F stationary in flat space under all variations of the surface and the slice. The area side is the drop of the stationary value of F in the perturbed spacetime, divided by 4G: an area deficit at fixed volume.

For conformal matter the two are equal at first order. This is the first law of causal diamonds (Jacobson and Visser, arXiv:1812.01596), and it is the content of Jacobson's derivation of the Einstein equation from entanglement equilibrium (arXiv:1505.04753). Gravitons first appear at second order in the metric perturbation, so for them the comparison is a second-order one: the first law of causal diamonds at second order.

On magnetic data the ratio of entropy to area side is (l+1)/(l+2) = 3/4, 4/5, 5/6, … for l = 2, 3, 4, …. That sequence is the ladder of the title. These statements are made at the stationary surface, in free linearised gravity at order G⁰ on a flat ball in four dimensions, under the assumptions listed in the status section.

How far each statement reaches

  • The entropy formula is exact to all orders, because the state is coherent and the theory is free. It has no cross term between the electric and magnetic sectors, since that would be odd under parity.
  • The area side on magnetic data is proved for every l.
  • The area side on electric data is computed exactly for l ≤ 12, and for every l given a closed form stated in the paper.
  • When both sectors are present, the area side is the sum of the two only if the ball is not pushed along a boost. For l = 2 that requires the electric and magnetic parts to share principal axes. This is argued from the symmetry of the zero modes; the general mixed case is not computed.

The entropy is blind to electric–magnetic duality. The area is not. No local Lorentz scalar can remove the mismatch: a scalar can move it between the two sectors, never cancel it. It is exactly the failure of the ball's conformal Killing vector to be a Killing vector. On the Rindler wedge, where the vector is Killing, the same computation gives no mismatch. The reason the entropy can be computed at all is that the modular group of the ball, the flow generated by its modular Hamiltonian, acts on the curvature as the conformal flow. Newer work: every graviton sector of the ball, both parities

Newer work in the record, version 4 of the paper in preparation

The two statements below are not in version 3 of the paper. They come from later work in the repository's working record and will be in version 4 of the paper, which is in preparation. Treat them as unreviewed: they carry every caveat listed in the status section.

  1. In every graviton sector of the flat ball, of either parity, the gauge-invariant fixed-volume area-side charge equals the vacuum's modular generator on metric data and exceeds it on extrinsic-curvature data by a positive term. The ladder (l+2)/(l+1) is one symptom of this, on stationary magnetic data.
  2. The extra term summed over sectors diverges logarithmically. The per-sector constant is 7ζ(3)/(16π²), about 0.0533, for odd parity (reproduced by a blind lattice run) and about 0.0136 for even parity (measured; the even divergence is computed, not yet proved).

In plain terms: metric data describe the shape of space on a slice, and extrinsic-curvature data describe how that shape is changing. The area-side charge and the vacuum modular generator agree on the first kind of data and differ on the second, always by a positive amount. The ladder ratio is what that difference looks like on stationary magnetic tides.

What is new and what was already in print

The area numbers at l = 2 are already in print: Jacobson, Senovilla and Speranza, Area deficits and the Bel–Robinson tensor, arXiv:1710.07379, eq. (75), at the constant-mean-curvature shape. They also showed that the area deficit is not the Bel–Robinson energy for any shape.

What we did not find in print is the entropy side, and so the comparison between the two. “Holds on electric data” is true at the stationary surface; for a geodesic ball the electric case fails too, while the magnetic coefficient is the same for every shape.

Status: read this before quoting the result

  • Proved here, with scriptsThe entropy formula (two routes), the area side on magnetic data, the area side on electric data from the exact Weyl solution, the wedge identity.
  • Assumed, and stated as assumptionsThe theory is free linearised gravity at order G⁰ on a flat ball in four dimensions; the gravitational term of the first law at this order is the stationary value of Area − 2·Volume; three conditions on what counts as “the algebra of the ball”; Einstein's equations to second order. The full list is in the paper, section 8, and in THEOREM.md, section 2.
  • OpenWhether the ball algebra of full quantum gravity reduces to the free one as G → 0. Nothing here tests that.
  • Not peer reviewedEvery check so far was made inside this programme. No outside referee has read the proofs.
  • Corrected once alreadyVersion 1 of the paper was public for about an hour before a blind read came back. It confirmed every formula and found four statements that were wrong or too broad, including that part of the area result is in print. Version 2 fixed them; version 3 only shortens the text. The read is in record/wave72/blindread/.
  • Not in version 3The sector-by-sector statements in the newer work section are from the working record and are not in the published paper. The even-parity divergence is computed, not yet proved.

Not in the repository yet

Reproduce it in one command

git clone https://github.com/ni1ra/graviton-ladder.git
cd graviton-ladder
pip install -r requirements.txt
./run_all.sh

This takes about three minutes on a laptop. Exit code 0 means every check passed and every planted error was caught: 461 checks and 109 planted errors behind the theorem, the electric-sector and Killing-limit scripts, and the blind reader's own scripts. FULL=1 ./run_all.sh also reruns the slow numerical search for the extremal surface, which takes about 30 minutes. The same command runs on every push; see the Actions tab.

All scripts use exact rational arithmetic (sympy) except four numerical checks and the lattice. The full statement and proof are in THEOREM.md, with a ledger of every premise next to it.

How to break it

The result would be refuted by any of these, and we would like to hear about it:

  1. An error in the proof that the modular flow of the helicity-2 field on a ball is the conformal flow (REPORT.md), or in Haag duality for the ball (REPORT.md).
  2. A second-order first law in print that balances on magnetic data with the same area prescription.
  3. A demonstration that the stationary surface for magnetic data is not the one used.
  4. A script here that passes when it should fail. Each one contains planted errors it must catch; if you find a wrong variant it does not catch, that is a finding.

Open an issue with three headings: Why (what you think is wrong), What (the step, equation or script), Where (file and line). Corrections are recorded, not erased: see REPORT.md for examples in our own work.

How this was made

The derivations, computations and checks were carried out by AI agents (Anthropic's Claude models) under the direction of the author, who set the questions, the scope and the rules. The record says which agent did what. The working rules were: outcome bins registered before each run; every script must catch a planted error; a result is credited only through content that was read and rerun; blind checkers are told to break the result; a second method counts for more than a second reader.

This is not a substitute for peer review, and one limit should be stated plainly: all checkers belong to one family of models and can share blind spots. That is why the scripts, the hypotheses and the failure conditions are published together.
