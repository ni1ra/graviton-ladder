# The graviton ladder

**Entropy cannot tell electric tides from magnetic ones. Area can.**

Take a ball in flat space and put a weak tidal gravitational field through it. Two things respond at second order in the field:

- the **entropy side**: the relative entropy of the gravitons inside the ball (how well an observer confined to the ball's causal diamond can tell the tidal field from empty space);
- the **area side**: the drop of the stationary value of Area − 2·Volume, divided by 4G.

The first law of causal diamonds, carried to second order, would make them equal. This repository contains an exact computation of both in free linearised gravity, with every check rerunnable in one command.

| Tidal field of multipole order l | Entropy | Area side | First law at second order |
|---|---|---|---|
| static electric | l + 1 | l + 1 | holds |
| stationary magnetic | l + 1 | l + 2 | misses by one part in l + 2 |
| (Rindler wedge, any data) | equal rates | equal rates | no miss |

For a uniform tidal field in a ball of radius R (l = 2, ħ = c = 1) the entropy is the Bel–Robinson super-energy of the ball:

$$S=\frac{\pi R^{6}}{420\,G}\left(\mathcal E_{ij}\mathcal E_{ij}+\mathcal B_{ij}\mathcal B_{ij}\right)$$

while the area side is $\frac{\pi R^{6}}{420\,G}\,\mathcal E_{ij}\mathcal E_{ij}$ for a purely electric field and $\frac{\pi R^{6}}{420\,G}\cdot\tfrac43\,\mathcal B_{ij}\mathcal B_{ij}$ for a purely magnetic one.

The entropy is blind to electric–magnetic duality. The area is not. No local Lorentz scalar can remove the mismatch (a scalar can move it between the two sectors, never cancel it). It is exactly the failure of the ball's conformal Killing vector to be a Killing vector: on the Rindler wedge, where the vector is Killing, the same computation gives no mismatch.

**What is new and what is not.** The area numbers at l = 2 are already in print: Jacobson, Senovilla and Speranza, [arXiv:1710.07379](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07379), eq. (75) at the constant-mean-curvature shape. They also showed the area deficit is not the Bel–Robinson energy for any shape. What we did not find in print is the entropy side, and so the comparison. "Holds on electric data" is true at the stationary surface; for a geodesic ball the electric case fails too, while the magnetic coefficient is the same for every shape.

**Start here:** [`paper/ladder_short.pdf`](paper/ladder_short.pdf) (8 pages, version 3).

## Status

Read this before quoting the result.

- **Proved here, with scripts:** the entropy formula (two routes), the area side on magnetic data, the area side on electric data from the exact Weyl solution, the wedge identity.
- **Assumed, and stated as assumptions:** the theory is free linearised gravity at order G⁰ on a flat ball in four dimensions; the gravitational term of the first law at this order is the stationary value of Area − 2·Volume; three conditions on what counts as "the algebra of the ball"; Einstein's equations to second order. The full list is in the paper, section 8, and in [`record/wave69/final/THEOREM.md`](record/wave69/final/THEOREM.md), section 2.
- **Open:** whether the ball algebra of full quantum gravity reduces to the free one as G → 0. Nothing here tests that.
- **Not peer reviewed.** Every check so far was made inside this programme. No outside referee has read the proofs.
- **Corrected once already.** Version 1 of the paper was public for about an hour before a blind read came back. It confirmed every formula and found four statements that were wrong or too broad, including that part of the area result is in print. Version 2 fixed them; version 3 only shortens the text. The read is in [`record/wave72/blindread/`](record/wave72/blindread/REPORT.md).

### 1. What the ladder is a symptom of

On every graviton sector of the flat ball, of either parity, the gauge-invariant fixed-volume area-side charge equals the vacuum's modular generator on metric data and exceeds it on extrinsic-curvature data by a positive form: local for odd parity, non-local for even (the Dirichlet Green operator of the ball). The contrast is metric data against extrinsic-curvature data, not electric against magnetic; the ladder above is this statement restricted to stationary magnetic data. Exact checks are at l = 2 to 6. Folders: `even-tower`, `charge-id`, `subspace`, `jss-id`.

### 2. The statement

Work in one parity. Let M be the von Neumann algebra generated by the Weyl operators of the sector data in the vacuum representation, U⁰ the vacuum's modular flow on the one-particle data, and U^g the flow generated by the vacuum's generator plus g times the extra term (g = 1 is the area-side charge). Then for every g ≠ 0 and every t ≠ 0 the Bogoliubov map U^g(2πt) U⁰(−2πt) is implemented by no unitary on Fock space. Consequently no faithful normal semifinite weight on M, and in particular no faithful normal state, has modular automorphism at time t equal to the area-side flow. The obstruction is a sum over sectors that diverges logarithmically.

"Normal" matters: nothing is said about states in other representations.

Folders: `assembly` (the statement, its chain and its hypothesis list), `referee` (an adversarial read of it: it holds; one remark about sign conventions was false as written and is corrected there), `all-t` (every non-zero time, not only t = 1/2).

### 3. What it rests on

- **Proved in this record**, each step with a written proof whose core the lead checked by hand and a script the lead re-ran:
  - the bound on the extra term: `hinge-proof` (odd, Lemma A), `even-proof` (even);
  - a first-order lower bound on an explicit family, uniform in l: `odd-sign`, `even-passage`;
  - the second-order remainder on that family: `odd-remainder`, `even-remainder`;
  - the antilinear part of the map from first principles: `beta-def`;
  - the change of variables to the hyperbolic frame is an exact identity with coupling 1: `even-dict`;
  - density, domains and boundedness on the one-particle space: `even-density`;
  - a unitary implementer forces the map to be continuous, so Shale's criterion applies with no boundedness input: `assembly`, section C;
  - the certified constants, recomputed for both families by second codes that never saw the first ones' values: `even-second`, `odd-second`.
- **Standard theorems**, located with their hypotheses in secondary sources (lecture notes and review papers), not in the originals: Shale's criterion (necessity), Connes' cocycle theorem, the bounded perturbation theorem, the modular operator of a second-quantised algebra. Folders: `std-theorems`, `phys-id`.
- **Identifications that are physics and are not proved here:** what the ball's graviton algebra is; that the vacuum's modular generator on each sector is the form used (the literature has theorems for conformally covariant nets and for free massless fields of arbitrary helicity, none of which names linearised gravity; `phys-id` states the condition under which they apply. For the scalar field each sector reduces to, `modgen` proves the geometry exactly, reduces the vacuum's covariance to the Hislop-Longo theorem plus a factoriality fact, and confirms it to 23 digits in both slots at l = 2 and 3; for gravitons the record's own proof that the modular group of that net acts on curvature as the conformal flow ([`record/wave70/modflow/`](record/wave70/modflow/)) was attacked by a referee lane and holds as a statement about curvature smeared inside the double cone (`modflow-ref`, which also records a misprint and unfilled placeholders in that report); the reduction of each sector of that curvature to a scalar field, with the inner product scaled by 4(l−2)!/(l+2)!, is proved for every l and checked exactly at l = 2 to 8 (`chain-all-l`), and a referee lane told to break that proof found no break and added the locality lemma it needs on the ball (`chain-ref`); what remains is that this net is the ball's graviton algebra, which is a definition); that the area-side charge is the physically relevant one.

### 4. Numbers

The two constants below measure the same obstruction by a different yardstick than the statement in item 2: they are the per-sector constants of the affinity (fidelity) defect between the two flows' states, lim λ²(1 − A_l), which is what the lattices measured. The proof in item 2 goes through a Hilbert–Schmidt norm and does not use them.

- Odd parity: 7ζ(3)/(16π²) ≈ 0.0533 (`hinge-proof`), reproduced by a lattice run that was meant to be blind and was weakened in one way its report states (`hinge-blind`).
- Even parity: 49ζ(3)/(16π²) − 3β(4)/π² + G/8 − (ln 2)/4 ≈ 0.0135994 in the large-l limit model, with β(4) the Dirichlet beta value and G Catalan's constant (`even-const`; the reduction to a one-dimensional integral is the lane's own, the integral's closed form was re-checked independently to 20 digits). A pre-registered lattice measurement was inconclusive under its own gate; a secondary analysis, fixed mid-run after about three quarters of the data, gives 0.01359 with a spread of 0.3% (`even-blind`), within 0.1% of the closed form.
- The proved lower bounds in item 3 are far from sharp, and the divergence does not depend on the values of these constants.

### 5. Which ruler

On the ball's light cone, with the size rule the printed first-law proof uses, the area-side form exceeds the entropy for every non-zero cone curvature at every l, identically in both parities: the difference is the manifestly positive integral (1/4G)∮∫v³|σ₂ − σ₃|² dv, by one integration by parts (`ruler-l`; exact Gram checks at l = 2 to 6, after `ruler` at l = 2). The ratio has infimum 1, not attained, and no upper bound. Electric-magnetic duality forbids the cone rule any asymmetry common to both parities. So the contrast in item 1 belongs to the fixed-volume, maximal-slice rule. On the two static tides, switching that rule's ingredients one at a time (`ruler-why`, l = 2, 3, 4) shows that balance on metric data needs both the volume constraint and the extremal edge, while the excess on extrinsic-curvature data is set by the size rule alone. The maximal-slice ingredient was handled there by an argument, not computed. These folders build on a folder of the working record that is not published here, and `ruler-l` records a fault in `ruler`'s script that affects l ≥ 3 only.

### 6. Open

- The lower bounds bite only for l above about 15,000 times the coupling at t = 1/2. That suffices for the divergence of the tail, and is tested non-vacuously at a single l.
- The standard theorems have not been read in the original papers.
- The three physics identifications in item 3.

### 7. Not claimed

- That an assumption of Jensen, Sorce and Speranza fails for gravitons, that no equilibrium (KMS) state exists for a ball, or that the area-side flow has no thermal state in any representation.
- That the charge here is the one they define: `jss-id` examines the relation and leaves a gap.
- That any theorem of Satishchandran and Sorce is wrong: `ss-second` checks one statement of their graviton appendix in D = 4, 5 and 6 and withdraws two side statements made in `ss-tension`.

Two folders hold intermediate steps that later folders made unnecessary and are kept as they were: `even-limit` (the large-l limit of the even operator) and `even-nonzero` (non-vanishing in that limit). These reports cite sibling folders of the working record that are not published here (`hinge`, `hinge-e`, `gen-law`, `null-cone` and some earlier waves). The scripts were run on Windows with Python 3.14; a few need `python-flint` in addition to `requirements.txt`, and say so in their report.

## Reproduce

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
./run_all.sh
```

About three minutes on a laptop. Exit code 0 means every check passed and every planted error was caught: 461 checks and 109 planted errors behind the theorem, the electric-sector and Killing-limit scripts, and the blind reader's own scripts. `FULL=1 ./run_all.sh` also reruns the slow numerical search for the extremal surface (about 30 minutes). The same command runs on every push (see the Actions tab).

All scripts use exact rational arithmetic (sympy) except four numerical checks and the lattice.

## How to break it

The result would be refuted by any of these, and we would like to hear about it:

1. An error in the proof that the modular flow of the helicity-2 field on a ball is the conformal flow (`record/wave70/modflow/REPORT.md`), or in Haag duality for the ball (`record/wave69/duality/REPORT.md`).
2. A second-order first law in print that balances on magnetic data with the same area prescription.
3. A demonstration that the stationary surface for magnetic data is not the one used.
4. A script here that passes when it should fail. Each one contains planted errors it must catch; if you find a wrong variant it does not catch, that is a finding.

Open an issue with three headings: **Why** (what you think is wrong), **What** (the step, equation or script), **Where** (file and line). Corrections are recorded, not erased: see `record/wave72/decider/REPORT.md` for examples in our own work.

## How this was made

The derivations, computations and checks were carried out by AI agents (Anthropic's Claude models) under the direction of the author, who set the questions, the scope and the rules. The record says which agent did what. The working rules were: outcome bins registered before each run; every script must catch a planted error; a result is credited only through content that was read and rerun; blind checkers are told to break the result; a second method counts for more than a second reader.

This is not a substitute for peer review, and one limit should be stated plainly: all checkers belong to one family of models and can share blind spots. That is why the scripts, the hypotheses and the failure conditions are published together.
